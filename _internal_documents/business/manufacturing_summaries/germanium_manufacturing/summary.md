# Germanium Manufacturing Summary

## Purpose
Germanium manufacturing supports high-frequency electronics, infrared optics, and specialty semiconductor devices where mobility and optical response are critical.

## Hardware Perspective
- Raw material and refining: Source germanium from zinc ore byproducts, then purify using chlorination and zone refining.
- Crystal growth: Grow high-purity single crystals for wafer slicing and substrate preparation.
- Wafer processing: Perform lapping, polishing, cleaning, and defect inspection before device fabrication.
- Device integration: Build Ge or Ge-on-Si structures for photodetectors, RF components, and mixed-material chips.
- Quality controls: Track dislocation density, contamination levels, wafer flatness, and resistivity uniformity.

## Software Perspective
- Process control systems: Use MES and SCADA workflows to manage lot routing, recipe execution, and traceability.
- Statistical process control: Monitor key metrics with control charts for thickness, impurity concentration, and defect rates.
- Predictive maintenance: Apply model-based alerts for furnaces, polishers, and metrology tools.
- Yield analytics: Correlate inline metrology and test data to root-cause yield loss.
- Digital twins: Simulate crystal growth and thermal profiles to reduce trial-and-error runs.

## Typical Risks
- Material contamination during purification and crystal handling.
- Thermal stress causing wafer cracking or defect propagation.
- Process drift in polishing and etch steps.

## Recommended KPIs
- Crystal pull yield.
- Wafer breakage rate.
- Defect density per wafer.
- Final device electrical pass rate.
